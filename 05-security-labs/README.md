# Security Labs

Assessment and analysis work performed in isolated lab environments against
intentionally vulnerable targets. All testing was authorized and confined to
lab-assigned systems; no production or third-party systems were involved at any
point.

## About This Work

The labs in this section are adapted and substantially rewritten from
coursework I completed in virtualized training environments. The methodology,
analysis, and conclusions are my own. Environment-specific configuration,
addressing, and scenario material has been generalized so that no lab platform
content is reproduced here.

## Why This Section

Security work belongs in a support portfolio because the two overlap constantly
in practice. A help desk technician is often the first to see the signs of a
problem — the account locking out repeatedly, the machine behaving oddly after a
user clicked something, the service reachable that should not be. Knowing what
those look like, and knowing how to document them clearly enough that someone
else can act on them, makes for a better technician.

## Ethical Statement

Every technique documented here was performed against systems I was explicitly
authorized to test, inside a closed lab. Scanning or probing systems without
written authorization is illegal in most jurisdictions and is not something I do
or endorse.
